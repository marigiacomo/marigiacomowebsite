Today a piece of news surfaced on my Apple Stocks app watchlist under the ASML ticker, it was released yesterday, 8th of August, by very well known financial journals such as WSJ and others.  The headline was “ASML Holding Draws Fresh Attention After $400 Million Bet On Lithography Rival”, and my reaction was like, ok, wait a minute, lithography rival? What are we talking about?

The article claims that this company whose name seems to be Source Foundry, has received $500 million in two tranches of $100 and $400 million at a valuation of $5 billion, from an AI oriented hedge fund, Situational Awareness which was founded in 2024 by the AI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner. There are some reports about the startup being also backed by Sequoia Capital.

He was born in Germany, where he studied in Berlin before moving to New York where he studied mathematics, statistics and economics atColumbia University and graduated as valedictorian, which I’ve just discovered is an award for the top ranking students, usually the ones with the highest GPA, congratulations Leopold.

He worked for OpenAI from 2023 to 2024 in their “Superalignment” team before being fired over an alleged case of information leak, which he claims was unjustified.

The name of his hedge fund, Situational Awareness, comes from his most famous paper about the emerging Artificial General Intelligence’s (AGI) capabilities and the related risks.

Leopold probably didn’t sleep well during the last months, the media reported that his fund was on the edge of a financial crisis at the end of July 2026, that led to a liquidation of it positions which involved Citadel’s founder to buy a part of the fund’s public equity portfolio.

It must be mentioned that neither Situational Awareness, nor Source Foundry nor Sequoia Capital, confirmed or even commented the news.

So, now that we know what Situational Awareness is and who its founder is, why should ASML draw fresh attention? Should such a colossus on which the industry built its pillars be scared of the possible competition?

Well I’m sure that Intel’s former CEO Andy Grove would have answered yes. On the 1st of September 1996 he published a book called “Only The Paranoid Survive” in which he describes how crucial paranoia has been during his career at Intel to keep the company aware of threats and prepared to take hits, in an era of innovative disruption and ruthless competition. That book is currently on the way to my mailbox and it will be one of my next readings.

That was in general terms, because everything we know so far, is that Source Foundry aims to tackle the lithography industry process to produce chips for the AI industry more efficiently.  It’s still unclear which part of the process they want to attack and if they have papers, research or developed technology to serve the purpose.

Something else that is unverified yet are the founders’ names, even though many journals report the names of Abdulmalik Obaid and Joe Burg.

Abdulmalik Obaid seems to be a PhD-level researcher in Stanford's Department of Materials Science and Engineering, he was awarded funding by the Bio-X graduate fellowship in 2018, and he’s the author of several papers, the most cited is “Massively parallel microwire arrays integrated with CMOS chips for neural recording” which sounds like an alien form of communication to me.

About Joe Burg, I am not certain that what I found is related to the Joe Burg who is said to be the co founder of this startup, anyway, he seems to be a PhD-level researcher at Standford as well, and his interests are “to develop computational methods to address the fundamental relationships between molecular structure and resulting mechanical properties of organosilicate glasses”, as it appears on his researcher page.

Their group affiliations during the PhD were respectively with Professor Nicholas Melosh and Reinhold Dauskardt who are both in Stanford’s Materials Science and Engineering department.

Now we know that the co-founders, considering these people I found are the actual ones, could have the capabilities and knowledge to develop something.

What I think is still missing to declare this startup a player in the industry is:

Real proof that these people or future employees and partners have the know how to also build and not just ideate these new disruptive machines, 

Real traction in the market, clients, orders and the capability to source the materials through their supply chain and fulfill customers’ requests,

Real money invested. I’m not an expert in the field but it took around 30 years and several billion to create the tech giant that left the others many years behind in innovation, ASML.
